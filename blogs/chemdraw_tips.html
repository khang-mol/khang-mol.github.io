As a general rule of thumb, your workflow with Chemdraw (or any other software for that matter) should increase in speed the less you rely on your mouse and the more you utilize the keyboard. The following will show you some tips to boost your productivity. (Since I use MacOS, I will talk about the "Command" ("Cmd") key. Generally, Windows will use the "Control" key instead.)

Tips inside the user interface

Here I will discuss some tips you can do when interacting solely through your user interface (UI). No coding-like knowdledge is needed.

Magic Hotkeys Cheat Sheet

This is a must have which you probably already knew. Click "Help" in the Menu Bar at the top of your screen and go to "Shortcuts and Hotkeys".

More Elaborate Cheat Sheet

To take this one step further, a more elaborate Cheat Sheet can be found here. It includes Shortcuts for aligning objects, cleaning up or joining structures, 3D clean-ups and more.

Automatic Chemical Formula Style

When you type chemical formulae with the text tool, you need subscripts and superscripts. A known method to apply these is by selecting the "Formula" button (or by pressing "Cmd + F") as shown in the picture below.

If you want this option as the default, go to "File" > "Document Settings..." > "Text Captions". In the Section "Baseline Style, click "Formula" and then "Ok".

More advanced tips

These tips will involve more than the UI. While no knowdledge of coding is necessary, it will certainly help.

Create new hotkeys/shortcuts via Settings (MacOS)

Click the Apple symbol in the top left corner and then "System Settings" (old: "System Preferences..."). In the sidebar, go to "Keyboard" > "Shortcuts" (or similar). While the image below is from an older MacOS version, you should get a window similar to this:

Select "App Shortcuts" on the left, click on the "+" symbol, then choose your specific Application (i.e. Chemdraw). Inside "Menu Title", enter the exact name of the menu command you want to add, including elipses (...) or similar. Every thing that is highlighted on your way to the command should be connected with "->". For the example below, I chose the command to distribute your selected objects horizontally. Since your need to hover over "Object", then "Distribute" and finally "Horizontally", the line that you should type in "Menu Title" is "Object->Distribute->Horizontally". Finally, assign a convenient Keyboard Shortcut of your choice. Just make sure, that it doesn't conflict with another Shortcut. To remove a Shortcut, click on the "−" symbol.

Create new hotkeys/shortcuts via xml

To create molecules in 3D, I like to incorporate the tool in the picture below into my workflow. First, select the "3DTrackball" tool highlighted by the orange arrow. Then, select the parts of the structure I want to change the perspective of (for multiple selections, press "Shift" while dragging with the mouse). Drag with your mouse until you are satisfied (press "Alt+Shift" to keep things steady). Add/Remove substituents and finally rotate to get part of your final product. That is how I managed to draw the commonly referenced drug vancomycin.
